% ----------------------------------------------------------------- CONTRIBUTIONS
\paragraph{Contributions.}
We extend the Sequential Keypoint Density Estimator (SeeKer) and frame our claims
strictly around what the evidence supports:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scene-SKDE: a multi-agent keypoint density.} We generalise
  SeeKer's autoregressive density from a \emph{single} human skeleton to a
  \emph{joint scene} of road agents. Each vehicle is encoded as a compact
  oriented-keypoint set (four oriented corners, centre, and a heading point,
  obtained from a YOLOv11-segmentation mask) and concatenated with the pedestrian
  skeleton, so all $N'{=}18{+}6M$ keypoints are modelled under one factorisation
  with the pedestrian predicted \emph{given} the surrounding vehicles. To our
  knowledge this is the first multi-agent extension of this estimator.
  \item \textbf{Context-conditioned variant with FiLM covariance.} As a lighter
  alternative we inject the vehicle geometry as a per-frame context (a per-class
  proximity vector, or the richer oriented ``car matrix'') through an
  autoregressive-preserving shortcut, and add a FiLM head that lets the context
  modulate the predicted covariance --- a concrete realisation of the proposal's
  ``contextual covariance penalty''.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that the gain is interaction, not object presence.}
  Through a counterfactual probe --- re-scoring each frame with the vehicle
  ``removed'' (context zeroed) --- we isolate the vehicle's effect on the
  pedestrian likelihood and show it is a configuration-dependent
  \emph{interaction} signal that the pedestrian-only baseline structurally cannot
  produce: the isolated interaction term reaches a near-vehicle-hazard AUROC of
  $0.97$ and lifts the score--proximity correlation from $0.16$ to $0.70$.
  \item \textbf{Honest, multi-seed evaluation.} We report mean$\pm$std over five
  seeds, a hazard-subset analysis, and a careful negative result: the
  \emph{overall}-AUROC advantage is operating-point-sensitive --- it narrows
  under temporal score smoothing --- so we claim a pedestrian-vehicle
  \emph{interaction} signal, not a global AUROC improvement.
\end{itemize}

% --------------------------------------------------------- RESULTS: PARAGRAPHS
\paragraph{Overall AUROC, with error bars.}
Across five seeds the baseline's per-epoch \emph{mean} AUROC is a tight
$0.725\pm0.004$, whereas its per-seed \emph{best} swings by $\pm0.018$ (range
$0.735$--$0.785$). This alone is instructive: single-seed ``best'' comparisons of
$\sim$1\,pp --- on which earlier skeleton-VAD reproductions often rest --- lie
\emph{entirely within} this epoch-to-epoch noise, so we compare on the stable
\emph{mean}. The car-matrix model attains a mean of $\PE{veh-mean}$, a
$\PE{dvb}$\,pp change over baseline; given the $\pm0.004$ baseline spread this is
\PE{significant/within noise}.

\paragraph{Is the spatial alignment real? A shuffled-context control.}
Permuting the context across segments (identical parameter count, but
geometry deliberately misaligned with the pedestrian) gives a mean of
$\PE{shuf-mean}$. The aligned-minus-shuffled gap is $\PE{dvs}$\,pp; it
\PE{exceeds / falls within} the seed-level noise, so we \PE{retain / soften} the
claim that the \emph{spatial alignment} of the context --- not merely the extra
structured input --- carries the signal.

\paragraph{The interaction signal (our primary result).}
Overall AUROC mixes motion-intrinsic anomalies (running, fighting) with the
context-dependent ones we target, and therefore understates the contribution. We
isolate the latter with a counterfactual: scoring each frame with the real
context and again with the context zeroed (vehicle ``removed''). Zeroing collapses
the model to near-baseline behaviour (full AUROC $0.741$, vehicle-hazard $0.79$,
score--proximity $\rho{=}0.15$). The isolated interaction term (real minus
counterfactual) is, in contrast, a near-perfect \emph{vehicle-hazard} detector
(AUROC $0.967$, $\rho{=}0.70$) yet only a weak \emph{general} detector
(full AUROC $0.69$) --- the signature of a configuration-dependent interaction
signal rather than a constant ``object-present'' offset. A class-balanced
within-near-vehicle AUROC (bootstrap) of $0.62\,[0.59,0.66]$ confirms the model
separates dangerous from benign near-vehicle frames above chance even after
removing the $\sim$91\%\ positive-label imbalance.

\paragraph{An honest caveat: operating-point sensitivity.}
ShanghaiTech AUROC is sensitive to temporal smoothing of the per-frame score. At
the baseline's best operating point ($\sigma{=}20$) the baseline rises to
$0.757$ while the car-matrix model --- whose interaction signal is temporally
\emph{punctate} --- falls to $0.765$, so the \emph{overall}-AUROC gap narrows to
$\sim$1\,pp. We report this rather than tuning the operating point in our favour:
the defensible claim is comparable overall AUROC \emph{plus} a distinct
interaction signal, not a global AUROC win.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
The density models are lightweight: $2.24$/$2.97$/$6.22$\,M parameters
(baseline / car-matrix$+$FiLM / scene) with single-batch forward latencies of
$0.30$/$0.41$/$0.72$\,ms ($>\!3\times10^{7}$ frame-scores/s) on one RTX~5080.
Perception (YOLO-seg, AlphaPose) is a one-time offline cost, adding nothing at
train or inference time.
