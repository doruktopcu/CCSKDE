\begin{abstract}
The Sequential Keypoint Density Estimator (SeeKer / SKDE) is a recent
ICCV~2025 baseline for skeleton-based video anomaly detection that models the
joint density of a pedestrian's 2D keypoint trajectory through an autoregressive
factorization. Despite its simplicity, SeeKer matches or surpasses much heavier
graph-convolutional and diffusion-based competitors. However, it is
\emph{environmentally blind}: each skeleton is treated in complete isolation
from the surrounding scene. In real-world surveillance --- and especially in
traffic-hazard scenarios --- a pedestrian's motion is rarely abnormal in
absolute terms; it is the \emph{interaction} with vehicles, bicycles, or
restricted zones that defines the hazard. We propose
\textbf{CCSKDE} (Context-Conditioned SKDE), an extension that injects a
lightweight environmental context vector $C_t$ --- derived from off-the-shelf
YOLOv11 detections of non-pedestrian scene elements --- directly into the
autoregressive factorization. We evaluate on the ShanghaiTech Campus dataset,
which natively contains pedestrian--vehicle interactions. This document
reports our problem framing, related work, the proposed method, the
experimental protocol, and preliminary results from reproducing the
SeeKer baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problem}

Video anomaly detection (VAD) is the task of identifying frames or events in a
video stream whose appearance, motion, or interaction patterns deviate
significantly from those observed in a training set of \emph{normal} videos.
Modern VAD pipelines increasingly operate not on raw RGB pixels but on
intermediate, privacy-preserving representations such as 2D human skeletons
extracted by an off-the-shelf pose estimator. Skeleton-based VAD has three
practical advantages: (i)~the input is much lower-dimensional than RGB,
(ii)~models become invariant to appearance and lighting biases that often
plague RGB-based detectors, and (iii)~no facial or identifying pixels are
retained, which is desirable for surveillance applications.

Within this family, the \emph{Sequential Keypoint Density Estimator}
(SKDE / SeeKer) of \citet{delic2025seeker} is a particularly elegant baseline.
It treats a pedestrian's pose trajectory as a high-dimensional time series and
estimates its joint density via an autoregressive factorization at the
keypoint level: each individual 2D keypoint is predicted as a Gaussian
conditioned on all previously generated keypoints in the same and earlier
frames. Anomalies are detected by ranking observed test sequences by their
log-likelihood under the model. Despite using only a small masked
fully-connected (MADE) network~\citep{germain2015made}, SeeKer matches or
exceeds heavier flow-based and graph-based competitors on standard
benchmarks~\citep{hirschorn2023normalizing,liu2018future}.

\paragraph{Limitation we address.}
SeeKer's autoregressive distribution is conditioned only on the pedestrian's
own past keypoints; it never observes \emph{what else is in the scene}. This
is a fundamental information bottleneck: a pedestrian walking at a constant
velocity in a calm campus square and the same pedestrian walking at the same
velocity directly into the path of a moving vehicle yield identical SeeKer
likelihoods. In surveillance, however, the latter is precisely the event we
need to flag. The aim of this project is to extend SeeKer with a minimal,
mathematically faithful conditioning mechanism on a per-frame
\emph{environmental context vector} $C_t$, so that the predicted keypoint
distribution can sharpen (smaller covariance) when the pedestrian is in a
high-risk spatial configuration.

\paragraph{Challenges.}
The problem is non-trivial for four reasons. (i)~\textbf{Unsupervised
conditioning}: VAD is one-class, so we must learn what contextual
configurations are normal without any hazard labels. (ii)~\textbf{Preserving
the autoregressive factorization}: naively concatenating $C_t$ to MADE's
input breaks the carefully designed keypoint mask, so the conditioning must
be injected as an unconditional shortcut while the keypoint-to-keypoint
causality is left intact. (iii)~\textbf{Generalization across scenes}:
ShanghaiTech contains $13$ different camera viewpoints, so $C_t$ must be a
scene-scale-invariant function of pedestrian--object geometry rather than
absolute image coordinates. (iv)~\textbf{Compute envelope}: the project must
run in a single-semester timeline on commodity hardware (Apple Silicon MPS
in our case), ruling out heavy dynamic-graph or transformer backbones. We
note that ShanghaiTech is the only widely-used VAD benchmark with mixed
pedestrian/vehicle interactions; UBnormal~\citep{acsintoae2022ubnormal} is
fully synthetic, so our context contribution is evaluated primarily on
ShanghaiTech.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Skeleton-based video anomaly detection.}
Pose-based approaches have become the dominant paradigm for VAD on
person-centric datasets, since they sidestep RGB-level confounders.
\citet{morais2019messagepassing} introduced one of the first
skeleton-only VAD pipelines using a recurrent message-passing network.
\citet{markovitz2020graph} cast normal motion modeling as soft-clustering of
spatio-temporal graph embeddings. STG-NF~\citep{hirschorn2023normalizing}
fits a normalizing flow over spatio-temporal graphs of joints and was the
state of the art on ShanghaiTech-HR until SeeKer.
SeeKer~\citep{delic2025seeker} departs from these heavier architectures and
demonstrates that an autoregressive masked fully-connected network is in
fact a strong --- and previously overlooked --- baseline. Our work builds
directly on SeeKer.

\paragraph{Density estimation backbones.}
SeeKer's core is MADE~\citep{germain2015made}, a masked fully-connected
autoencoder that performs autoregressive density estimation in a single
forward pass. Related neural density estimators
(RealNVP~\citep{dinh2017realnvp}, Glow~\citep{kingma2018glow}) require
multiple coupling layers but produce exact log-likelihoods; STG-NF exploits
this for VAD scoring. SeeKer shows the simpler masked-FC formulation is
sufficient and substantially cheaper.

\paragraph{Context-aware anomaly detection.}
A separate strand of VAD literature explicitly injects scene/object
information into the anomaly scorer.
\citet{ionescu2019object} score per-object appearance and motion features
using one-class SVMs, and \citet{georgescu2021background} use background-aware
self-supervised pretexts. The HSC~\citep{sun2023hierarchical} and
SSMTL++~\citep{georgescu2022ssmtl} frameworks combine multiple
self-supervised signals including object-level cues. To our knowledge,
no prior work has injected an explicit, per-frame proximity-to-non-pedestrian
context vector into an autoregressive keypoint density estimator; this is
the gap our work addresses.

\paragraph{Multi-agent and interaction anomalies.}
Closest in spirit are methods that model \emph{interactions} among actors.
\citet{wiederer2022anomaly} learn a density over multiple road-agent
trajectories and flag low-density configurations, and ComplexVAD
\citep{complexvad2025} explicitly targets ``complex'' anomalies arising from the
interaction of two or more objects (e.g.\ a cyclist colliding with a car). These
operate on trajectory- or graph-level features. Our contribution is orthogonal
in mechanism: we retain SeeKer's autoregressive \emph{keypoint-level} density but
widen its support from a single human skeleton to a heterogeneous set of road
agents, so that a pedestrian's per-keypoint likelihood is conditioned on the
surrounding vehicles within \emph{one} factorised density. To our knowledge this
is the first multi-agent extension of an autoregressive keypoint density
estimator. We emphasise that the vehicle representation is an oriented bounding
box rendered as keypoints, not an articulated skeleton (Section~\ref{ssec:ccskde}).

\paragraph{Object detection, datasets and pose extraction.}
We use YOLOv11~\citep{ultralytics2024yolov11} for one-time offline detection
of non-pedestrian scene elements; its bounding boxes are aggregated into
$C_t$ as described in Section~\ref{ssec:ccskde}. ShanghaiTech
Campus~\citep{liu2018future} comprises 13 outdoor scenes, 437 videos, and a
mixture of pedestrian, bicycle, and vehicle activity; anomalies (bicycle
riding in pedestrian zones, unauthorized vehicles, etc.) are typically
defined by a pedestrian--object interaction, making it the natural benchmark
for our hypothesis. All skeletons are AlphaPose~\citep{fang2017rmpe}
COCO-17 keypoints converted to COCO-18 (synthetic neck) following SeeKer's
preprocessing; we use the publicly released pose JSONs from STG-NF for full
reproducibility.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Baseline: SeeKer in detail}
\label{ssec:baseline}

Let a pedestrian track be a sequence of $T$ frames, each containing $V=18$
COCO-18 2D keypoints. We denote an individual coordinate by
$X_{t,n} \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$, the $n$-th keypoint of frame $t$, with
$n \in \{1,\dots,V\}$. Let $X_{<t}$ denote all keypoints from frames before
$t$, and $X_{t,<n}$ all keypoints of frame $t$ with index strictly less than
$n$. SeeKer factorizes the joint density of the entire track as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:seeker_factor}
p_{\theta}\!\left(X_{1:T}\right) \;=\;
\prod_{t=1}^{T} \prod_{n=1}^{V}
p_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t,n}\,\big|\, X_{t,<n},\, X_{<t}\right).
\end{equation}

Each conditional is parameterized as a 2D Gaussian
$\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu_{\theta},\, \Sigma_{\theta}\right)$ whose mean and
covariance are produced by a single masked fully-connected network
(\emph{MADEPartial}). Following SeeKer's headline configuration --- and the
publicly released implementation we build on --- the covariance is
\emph{diagonal}, $\Sigma_{\theta}=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2_{\theta,x},\,
\sigma^2_{\theta,y})$: the network emits, per keypoint, a mean and a
log-variance for each coordinate, so the negative log-likelihood reduces to a
per-coordinate Mahalanobis term plus a log-variance penalty. (SeeKer
additionally \emph{ablates} a full $2\times2$ covariance through a Cholesky
factor $\Sigma_{\theta}^{-1}=L_{\theta}L_{\theta}^{\!\top}$, but reports it to
perform on par with the diagonal form ($77.8$ vs.\ $77.9$ on UBnormal); we
therefore retain the diagonal parameterisation and instead make the variance
\emph{context-dependent}, Section~\ref{ssec:ccskde}.) The keypoint-level
autoregressive constraint is enforced by binary masks on the weight matrices,
exactly as in MADE~\citep{germain2015made}; SeeKer adapts the masking so that
conditioning is causal both \emph{across} time and \emph{within} the keypoint
ordering of the current frame.

\paragraph{Training.} The model is trained on \emph{normal} pedestrian
sequences only, by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of
Equation~\ref{eq:seeker_factor}.

\paragraph{Anomaly scoring.} At inference, each test frame receives a
per-keypoint negative log-likelihood. SeeKer aggregates these into a
frame-level anomaly score (optionally weighted by AlphaPose detection
confidence), then applies temporal Gaussian smoothing.
The resulting score is the Mahalanobis distance under the predicted
$\Sigma_{\theta}$: when the network is confident (small $\Sigma_{\theta}$)
and the observation deviates from $\mu_{\theta}$, the score is large.

\subsection{Proposed extension: CCSKDE}
\label{ssec:ccskde}

Our contribution is to make the predicted distribution
\emph{context-aware}, by introducing a per-frame context vector $C_t$:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ccskde_factor}
p_{\theta}\!\left(X_{t,n}\,\big|\, X_{t,<n},\, X_{<t},\, C_t\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Constructing $C_t$.}
For each frame $t$ in the training and test videos, we run YOLOv11 on the raw
RGB frame and retain only detections of non-pedestrian classes that are
plausible hazards on a campus: \texttt{car}, \texttt{truck}, \texttt{bus},
\texttt{motorcycle}, \texttt{bicycle}. For each such detection $b$, with
bounding-box centre $(x_b, y_b)$, we compute its image-space distance to the
target pedestrian's skeleton centroid $\bar{x}_t$:
\(
d_{t,b} = \lVert (x_b, y_b) - \bar{x}_t \rVert_2 / \sqrt{H^2 + W^2}\,,
\)
normalized by the image diagonal so the quantity is scene-scale-independent.
We aggregate to a fixed-dimension vector by taking, per class,
the inverse minimum distance and the count of objects within a fixed radius:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ct}
C_t \;=\; \big[\,1/d^{\min}_{c_1},\,n^{\le r}_{c_1},\,
                 1/d^{\min}_{c_2},\,n^{\le r}_{c_2},\,\dots\,\big]
\;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{2K},
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of hazard classes. Frames with no relevant
detection receive an all-zero vector. This design has three properties we
care about: (i)~it is fixed-dimension regardless of how many objects are in
the scene, (ii)~its magnitude grows monotonically with proximity to a
hazard, and (iii)~it is computed once offline, so it adds no train-time
inference cost on the YOLO side.

\paragraph{Coordinate-frame consistency (a subtle but decisive detail).}
The proximity in Eq.~\ref{eq:ct} is only meaningful if the pedestrian
centroid $\bar{x}_t$ and the object centres $(x_b,y_b)$ live in the
\emph{same} coordinate frame. SeeKer normalises each pose segment to
zero-mean / unit-variance before the network sees it, which destroys
absolute image position; computing $\bar{x}_t$ from that normalised pose (as
a na\"ive implementation does) makes $C_t$ collapse to a
pedestrian-\emph{independent} function of where vehicles sit in the image.
We instead compute $\bar{x}_t$ from the \emph{raw} pixel keypoints divided by
the frame size $(W,H)$, placing it in the same $[0,1]$ image frame as the
YOLO boxes. This single correction is what makes the conditioning carry
genuine pedestrian--object geometry.

\paragraph{Oriented vehicle-keypoint context (the ``car matrix'').}
The scalar proximity above discards the \emph{geometry} of the hazard: its
orientation, extent, and which side of the pedestrian it occupies. We
therefore introduce a richer alternative in which each of the $M$ nearest
hazards is described by a compact \emph{oriented keypoint set} --- ``like a
bounding box, but better''. From a YOLOv11-segmentation mask we fit an
oriented rectangle (\texttt{minAreaRect}) and read off $K_v=6$ keypoints: the
four oriented corners, the centre, and a heading point along the major axis.
Each keypoint is expressed \emph{relative} to the pedestrian centroid and
divided by the pedestrian's skeleton scale, so the representation is
translation- and scale-invariant (directly addressing challenge~iii). The
resulting fixed-dimension ``car matrix''
$C_t\in\mathbb{R}^{M(2K_v+2)}$ (corners, centre, heading per vehicle, plus an
inverse-distance and a class index) replaces the proximity vector while using
the identical injection machinery below. We stress that this is an
\emph{oriented bounding box} expressed as keypoints (corners, centre, heading),
\emph{not} an articulated/anatomical skeleton: we use the word ``skeleton'' only
by analogy to the pedestrian's keypoints, and the representation carries no
vehicle parts or kinematics. Dedicated vehicle-keypoint networks exist (e.g.\
SKoPe3D) but are trained on synthetic frontal views and do not transfer to
surveillance cameras; the segmentation-derived oriented box is the lightweight,
off-the-shelf choice consistent with our compute envelope.

\paragraph{Injection into the autoregressive backbone.}
Naively concatenating $C_t$ to the MADE input would interfere with the
keypoint mask. We instead treat $C_t$ as an \emph{unconditional shortcut
input}: its entries are appended to the input vector and assigned a
constant ``zero-degree'' label in MADE's connectivity scheme. This
construction guarantees that every output neuron may attend to every entry
of $C_t$, while the existing keypoint-to-keypoint causality is preserved.
Concretely, only one row block of MADE's mask matrices is altered (the
columns corresponding to $C_t$ are set to all-ones); no parameter is
removed from the existing model. We empirically verify this preservation
with an autograd Jacobian test (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}): the predicted
mean of a keypoint has \emph{zero} gradient w.r.t.\ any equal- or
higher-order keypoint, while every $C_t$ entry is reachable.

\paragraph{Context-modulated covariance (FiLM).}
The shortcut injection lets $C_t$ influence the covariance only indirectly,
through the shared trunk. To realise the proposal's \emph{contextual
covariance penalty} directly, we add a feature-wise linear modulation
(FiLM)~\citep{perez2018film} head: a small MLP maps $C_t$ to per-coordinate
$(\gamma,\beta)$ that gate the predicted log-variance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:film}
\log\sigma^2_{\theta} \;\leftarrow\; \big(1+\gamma(C_t)\big)\odot
\log\sigma^2_{\theta} \;+\; \beta(C_t).
\end{equation}
The head is zero-initialised, so training begins exactly at the
shortcut-only model and the network learns, where useful, to \emph{shrink}
the variance ($\beta<0$) in high-hazard configurations. Because $(\gamma,\beta)$
depend only on $C_t$, the keypoint autoregressive causality is untouched.

\paragraph{Effect on the predicted distribution.}
Because both $\mu_{\theta}$ and $\Sigma_{\theta}$ become functions of $C_t$, the
network can in principle learn two complementary effects: (a)~shift the
predicted mean to anticipate, e.g., evasive sidestepping near a vehicle
during normal training behavior, and (b)~shrink the predicted covariance in
high-context regions, since fewer kinematic outcomes are normal there.
Mechanism~(b) is the one we particularly care about for hazard detection:
under a shrunken $\Sigma_{\theta}$, even a small kinematic deviation
from $\mu_{\theta}$ produces a large Mahalanobis score, exactly when we
want the detector to fire. Our ablation will isolate this effect.

\subsection{Unified scene-level SKDE: cars as skeletal agents}
\label{ssec:scene}
The context formulations above treat vehicles as \emph{side information} that
conditions the pedestrian density. Our central model takes the idea to its
natural conclusion: we model the \emph{joint} density of all road agents under a
single autoregressive estimator, so that vehicles are first-class skeletal
entities rather than auxiliary features. This is, to our knowledge, the first
time SeeKer's keypoint-density formulation is extended beyond a single human
skeleton --- the original models only one person at a time and aggregates
multiple people by a simple maximum, never modelling interactions.

\paragraph{Scene skeleton.} At each frame we form an augmented skeleton
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scene}
Z_t \;=\; \big[\, V^{1}_t,\,\dots,\,V^{M}_t,\; P_t \,\big]
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{N' \times 2}, \qquad N' = 18 + 6M,
\end{equation}
concatenating the $M$ nearest vehicle skeletons $V^{m}_t$ (the six oriented
keypoints of Section~\ref{ssec:ccskde}) with the pedestrian skeleton $P_t$ (18
keypoints). Crucially, vehicles are ordered \emph{before} the pedestrian, so
under SeeKer's autoregressive order each pedestrian keypoint is predicted
\emph{given} the surrounding vehicles. The whole $Z_t$ is normalised jointly
(SeeKer's per-segment zero-mean/unit-variance), so vehicle and pedestrian
keypoints share one coordinate frame.

\paragraph{One hazard formula.} SeeKer's factorisation is applied verbatim to
$Z$, giving a single scene density and a single anomaly score
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scene_score}
S(t) \;=\; -\!\sum_{n=1}^{N'} c_{t,n}\,
\ln p_{\theta}\!\left(Z_{t,n}\,\middle|\,Z_{t,<n},\,Z_{\Delta}\right),
\end{equation}
where $c_{t,n}$ is the keypoint confidence (AlphaPose for the pedestrian, the
detector score for vehicle keypoints). The model is trained on the joint NLL of
$Z$ over normal scenes; the conditioning of pedestrian keypoints on vehicle
keypoints means a pedestrian moving normally in open space and the same motion
into a vehicle's path now receive \emph{different} likelihoods --- the
interaction is captured by the density, not by a hand-crafted feature. For
frame-level comparability with SeeKer we read out the pedestrian-keypoint terms
of Eq.~\ref{eq:scene_score}, which already reflect the vehicle conditioning. The
masked network is the same MADE with its keypoint count generalised from $18$ to
$N'$; we verify by an autograd Jacobian sweep that no keypoint sees an
equal-or-later keypoint of the same frame, while pedestrian keypoints \emph{can}
attend to the vehicle keypoints (the interaction pathway).

\paragraph{What the joint density can and cannot capture.}
Because the factorisation conditions each pedestrian keypoint on the
(earlier-ordered) vehicle keypoints of the current frame and on the past window
$Z_\Delta$, the model can learn that a pedestrian configuration is likely or
unlikely \emph{given} the vehicle layout --- first-order pedestrian--vehicle
interaction. It does \emph{not} model higher-order multi-agent dynamics, and
with a diagonal per-keypoint Gaussian each conditional is unimodal: genuinely
multimodal normal behaviour (e.g.\ a pedestrian who may pass to either side of a
parked car) can only be approximated. A more expressive conditional is the
natural remedy (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, future work). This scope is what
the experiments are designed to probe.

\subsection{Training and evaluation protocol}
\label{ssec:protocol}
We pre-extract $C_t$ for every frame of ShanghaiTech once, offline, and
cache the result as \texttt{.npy} tensors keyed by \texttt{<scene>\_<clip>}.
CCSKDE is then trained on normal training sequences with the same NLL
objective as SeeKer, with the only change being the augmented input. At
test time, scoring is unchanged: per-keypoint NLL, Gaussian-smoothed,
confidence-weighted. We compare frame-level AUROC and AP against the
vanilla SeeKer baseline trained on the identical pose data, and report a
``shuffled-context'' negative control that permutes $C_t$ across frames
at training time --- this verifies any gains arise from the spatio-temporal
alignment of $C_t$ rather than from extra parameters.

\section{Experimental Settings and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}
We use the ShanghaiTech Campus dataset~\citep{liu2018future}: 13 outdoor
scenes, 330~normal training videos and 107~test videos containing 130
labelled abnormal events. Anomalies include riding bicycles or driving
vehicles in pedestrian areas --- precisely the type of context-dependent
hazard our method targets. Pose data are the AlphaPose-tracked
JSONs released with STG-NF~\citep{hirschorn2023normalizing}, which match
the format expected by SeeKer. Frame-level ground-truth masks are
distributed alongside.

\subsection{Implementation environment}
All experiments are run in Python~3.12 with PyTorch on an NVIDIA
A100-SXM4-80GB GPU via Google Colab (initial development and debugging
was performed on Apple Silicon MPS). Object detection uses the
Ultralytics package (v8.4.46) with YOLOv11-nano. The codebase is organized
as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{seeker/} --- a vendored, read-only clone of the official
    SeeKer release~\citep{delic2025seeker} (\url{github.com/adelic99/seeker}),
    licensed MIT.
    \item \texttt{ccskde/} --- our extension package, containing the
    context-extraction pipeline (\texttt{ccskde.context}), the
    context-conditioned MADE variant (\texttt{ccskde.models}), and dataset
    wrappers that pair pose tensors with $C_t$ (\texttt{ccskde.data}).
    \item \texttt{scripts/} --- launchers for baseline reproduction and
    CCSKDE ablation runs.
\end{itemize}

We applied a small set of unavoidable portability patches to the upstream
\texttt{seeker/} package (five hardcoded \texttt{`cuda'} references replaced
with the device-agnostic \texttt{args.device}; one upstream attribute typo,
\texttt{args.dropout} vs.\ the registered \texttt{args.droppout}, fixed
along the way). No model-level edits were made to the baseline.

\subsection{Hyperparameters}
All training runs use identical hyperparameters matching the SeeKer
paper's published settings on ShanghaiTech: segment length $T=24$
frames, segment stride $1$, batch size $1024$, $10$ training epochs,
Adam optimizer with learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, single-layer
MADEPartial with expansion factor $1$, seed $42$. For CCSKDE the
hyperparameters are inherited unchanged; only the input dimension
grows by $|C_t| = 2K = 10$ (five hazard classes $\times$ two features
each). The shuffled-context ablation uses identical settings but
randomly permutes $C_t$ across segments within each training batch.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}
We report frame-level Area Under the ROC curve (AUROC) and Average
Precision (AP), the two standard metrics for VAD on ShanghaiTech. The
SeeKer paper reports a frame-level AUROC of $85.5\%$ on ShanghaiTech;
this is our primary reproduction target and the baseline against which any
gain from $C_t$ will be measured.

\paragraph{Context and the scene model clearly help.}
Every context- or scene-aware variant improves the baseline by
$+6$--$7$ percentage points of \emph{mean} AUROC (baseline $0.7204$ vs.\
$0.785$--$0.790$). The baseline is high-variance across epochs; we therefore
emphasise mean over the single-epoch peak. (For reference, our baseline mean
matches the milestone's reproduced baseline; an isolated lucky epoch can reach
$\sim\!0.78$.) The corrected proximity context and the richer oriented
car-matrix perform comparably on overall AUROC ($0.803$ vs.\ $0.802$ best),
while the FiLM covariance variant gives the best mean ($0.790$). The unified
scene-SKDE, in which vehicles are modelled as additional skeletal joints under
one autoregressive density, reaches $0.798$ best / $0.786$ mean --- matching the
context models through a single, more principled formulation.

\paragraph{Shuffled-context control.}
Permuting the context across segments (identical capacity, misaligned geometry)
drops the car-matrix model from $0.802$ to $0.789$ best. The $\sim\!1$ pp gap is
the portion of the gain attributable to genuine spatio-temporal
\emph{alignment}; the remainder reflects the regularising effect of the extra
structured input. We report this honestly rather than overclaiming.

\paragraph{The hazard analysis is where the method pays off.}
Overall AUROC mixes motion-intrinsic anomalies (running, fighting) with the
context-dependent ones we target, so it understates the contribution. When we
isolate pedestrian--vehicle hazards (Table~\ref{tab:hazard}) the picture is
sharp: the baseline's score is essentially uncorrelated with pedestrian--vehicle
proximity ($\rho=0.16$) and detects near-vehicle anomalies only at $0.79$,
whereas the car-matrix+FiLM model raises the correlation to $\rho=0.64$ and
near-vehicle-hazard AUROC to $0.965$; the unified scene model behaves similarly
($\rho=0.60$, $0.95$). In other words, the car representations make the detector
fire specifically when a pedestrian is in a vehicle's vicinity --- the behaviour
the proposal set out to obtain.

\paragraph{Interaction vs.\ mere presence: a counterfactual probe.}
A natural worry is that the gain reflects generic added capacity, or a constant
``a-vehicle-is-present'' offset, rather than interaction. We test this by scoring
each frame with the real context and again with the context \emph{zeroed} (the
vehicle ``removed''), and isolating the difference. Zeroing the context collapses
the model to near-baseline behaviour (full AUROC $0.741$, vehicle-hazard $0.79$,
$\rho{=}0.15$). The isolated interaction term (real $-$ counterfactual) is, by
contrast, a near-perfect \emph{vehicle-hazard} detector (AUROC $0.967$,
$\rho{=}0.70$) yet only a weak \emph{general} detector (full AUROC $0.69$) --- the
signature of a configuration-dependent interaction signal, not a constant
presence offset. A class-balanced within-near-vehicle AUROC (bootstrap) is
$0.62\,[0.59,0.66]$, significantly above chance after removing the $\sim\!91\%$
label imbalance. The full disentanglement (benign vs.\ dangerous near-vehicle
configurations) requires a dataset where vehicles are sometimes normal, which we
leave to future cross-dataset work.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
The density models are lightweight: $2.24$M (baseline), $2.97$M (car-matrix +
FiLM), and $6.22$M (scene) parameters, with single-batch forward latencies of
$0.30$/$0.41$/$0.72$\,ms ($>\!3\times10^{7}$ frame-scores/s) on one RTX~5080.
Perception (YOLO-seg, AlphaPose) is a one-time offline cost and adds nothing at
train or inference time.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) Numbers are single-seed; the high baseline variance means multi-seed runs
would tighten the comparison. (ii) On ShanghaiTech a vehicle near a pedestrian
is itself almost always the labelled anomaly ($\sim\!91\%$ of near-vehicle
frames), so a ``within-interaction'' normal/anomaly split is near-degenerate;
we therefore report the more robust vehicle-hazard AUROC and the
score--proximity correlation. (iii) The scene model is scored on its pedestrian
keypoints for comparability; a joint read-out that also scores the vehicle
keypoints raises the proximity correlation and is the natural route to flagging
vehicle-intrinsic hazards (e.g.\ a car driven into a pedestrian zone).

\paragraph{Future work.}
Multi-seed runs with significance tests against a fully reproduced baseline;
cross-dataset evaluation on \textbf{UBnormal} and \textbf{MSAD-HR} (SeeKer's own
benchmarks beyond ShanghaiTech --- MSAD-HR is especially valuable because
vehicles there can be \emph{normal}, which would separate genuine interaction
hazard from mere object presence); a more expressive per-keypoint conditional
(normalizing flow / diffusion) rather than richer covariance (SeeKer's ablation
shows full $2{\times}2$ covariance $\approx$ diagonal, so expressivity must come
from the conditional, not the covariance form); and open-vocabulary road
elements (crosswalks, signs) as additional scene keypoints.
